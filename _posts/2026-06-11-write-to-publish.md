One idea that keeps getting more true for me is this:

writing is not just a way to express what you know. Writing is one of the main ways you come to know what you know.

A lot of us treat writing like the final packaging step. First you learn, then you think, then maybe later you write something polished for other people.

I think that is backwards.

Writing is the medium that forces thought to become visible. Until you can explain something clearly in your own words, you probably do not understand it as well as you think you do. You might recognize it. You might be able to nod along to it. You might even be able to repeat the original source. But that is different from owning the idea.

Real understanding shows up when you can restate the thing cleanly.

That is why I like the phrase write to publish.

I do not mean literally publish every note. Most notes should stay private. I mean write every note as if it could eventually be useful to another person, even if that other person is only future you.

Write it clearly. Write it completely enough that your future self is not confused. Write it in your own language instead of copying the source. If a note is vague, shorthand-heavy, or dependent on your current mood to make sense, it is not really finished.

Unclear notes are a kind of hidden debt.

I wrote last week about why [information management matters more than time management](/2026/06/04/information-management-matters-more-than-time-management.html). This is the same idea from the other side. A good note system helps you find and reconnect ideas. But the system cannot save a note that never became clear in the first place.

Writing does several jobs at once:

- it exposes weak spots in your understanding
- it forces you to choose what actually matters
- it makes ideas easier to connect across subjects
- it creates reusable building blocks for future work

This applies to almost everything. Technical concepts. Business ideas. Math. Personal lessons. It is the same reason I started [this blog with Jekyll and GitHub Pages](/2026/02/22/starting-a-blog-in-5-minutes.html) instead of keeping every thought in Obsidian forever. If I cannot explain something simply, I should assume I still have work to do.

There is also a psychological benefit. Writing turns fuzzy thinking into an object you can inspect. Once the idea is on the page, you can improve it. Before that, it just floats around in your head pretending to be more coherent than it is.

That is why good writing and good learning feel so close to each other. They are not separate activities. In a lot of cases they are the same activity seen from different angles.

So when I say write to publish, what I really mean is this:

write like clarity matters.

Write like your future self will need this.

Write like the act of putting it into words is part of the learning, not just the reporting.
